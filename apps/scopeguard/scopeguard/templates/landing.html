Scope you can point back to

Deliverables, exclusions, milestones, deadlines and revision allowances. Publishing a version freezes it as a timestamped snapshot that is never edited; changes become a new version, with a diff against the last one.

Change requests with a clear status

Each request records what will change, the reason, and an optional time and price impact. ScopeGuard never fills those in for you. A request moves from draft to submitted to approved or rejected, and once submitted it is frozen.

A review page for your client

Send a private link issued to one named person, either as an approval link or as view-only. They review the scope on any phone or computer, approve it or ask for changes, and approve or reject change requests. No client account needed.

Revisions and history

Revision requests are counted against each deliverable's allowance, with a warning when it's used up. Every decision and change goes into an append-only history.

Printable record and exports

A printable summary shows the original agreed scope next to approved changes. Export everything as JSON or CSV.

Messages you send yourself

Editable message templates are filled in per project for you to copy. ScopeGuard never sends email.

What an approval is, and isn't

Client approvals are typed-name acknowledgements, recorded with a timestamp, IP address and browser. They are not handwritten or certified electronic signatures, and every record is labelled that way. An approval click is a record of agreement, not a legal electronic signature. ScopeGuard documents agreements; it is not legal advice and does not guarantee that an agreement or approval is legally enforceable.
